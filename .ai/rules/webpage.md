# Webpage

## Storefront HTML is also cached at the Cloudflare edge
Since 31 Oct 2026 (trial on www.awgifts.eu, every live storefront zone from 15:00 UTC) a Cloudflare cache rule stores anonymous storefront HTML at the edge (72 hours since 3 Nov 2026, 24 hours from 1 Nov, 10 minutes before; a 404 for 1 hour since 3 Nov 2026, so scrapers probing old file names are answered at the edge, while every other non-200 is never stored), with Tiered Cache (Smart topology) on so one copy near our servers serves every Cloudflare location (bypassed by the iris_vua cookie, X-Inertia, Authorization, static extensions and the Varnish pass paths; X-Aiku-Ssr-Fallback and JSON responses are never stored). In Cloudflare rule expressions `http.request.headers.names[*]` keeps the client's case, so header checks must be `any(lower(http.request.headers.names[*])[*] eq "x-inertia")`: a plain `eq "x-inertia"` let an HTTP/1.1 `X-Inertia` request store Inertia JSON as the page. Every Varnish ban path (BanVarnishWebpage, PurgeVarnishWebpageUrl, PurgeVarnishPath, BreakWebsiteVarnishCache, UpdateWebpageCanonicalUrl, the master price/trade-unit pool bans and varnish:restart) calls `PurgeCloudflareWebsitePages::schedule()`, which books one Cloudflare purge of the website's pages 10 minutes later and lets every other ban in those 10 minutes ride on it (a `Cache::add` lock that lives exactly as long as the delay; ShouldBeUnique would drop bans landing mid-purge). So an edit reaches anonymous visitors within about 10 minutes whatever the edge TTL. That covers a cached 404 too: publishing a page runs BreakWebpageCache, so a URL that was a 404 shows the new page within about 10 minutes, not the hour. The exception is price and stock (Raul, 2 Nov 2026): a product change that touches only `price`, or only stock (the in-stock flip and its `status` move between for-sale, out-of-stock and coming-soon, written by ProductHydrateAvailableQuantity in the same update), and a master cascade of prices alone, ban Varnish but do not purge the edge (`BanVarnishWebpage` / `BreakProductInWebpagesCache` with `purgeEdge: false`). Logged-out visitors can therefore see stock up to 72 hours old (the back-in-stock Remind me button, the in-stock-first order of family and collection grids, See also) and a stale price in the JSON-LD and page data; Raul accepted that, and prices and stock will be removed for logged-out visitors. Before this, weekday stock flips wiped every busy site at the edge every 10 minutes and the 24 h copy hit no more than the old 10-minute one (26% of cacheable requests on Monday 2 Nov vs 39% on Sunday). Offers are shown only to logged-in visitors (every live template gates DiscountByType on is_logged_in; ProductCardEcom2, the products-2 card, does not, but no live page uses it on 2 Nov 2026), so the offer side effects (HandlesOfferSideEffects::cleanWebpagesCache) call BreakWebpageCache with `purgeEdge: false` too, which reaches PurgeVarnishWebpageUrl, the family listing pages and the child product pages. A live page switching to products-2 would show stale offer badges to logged-out visitors. RRP is not in logged-out page data since HELP-4214 (21 Oct 2026: HideRetailPricesFromIrisVisitors strips rrp, rrp_per_unit and the profit fields server-side, so templates that would render an RRP show nothing), so since 4 Nov 2026 RRP changes do not purge either (UpdateProduct, and the master price/RRP cascade, which books no purge at all); status moves to or from discontinued, not-for-sale or in-process still purge. Every purge that reaches Cloudflare writes a `website_edge_purges` row (since 4 Nov 2026): `trigger` (scheduled, manual or deploy), `bans` and `ban_sources`, the count of bans since the previous purge keyed by the chain of aiku classes that called `schedule()`/`purgeNow()` (e.g. `UpdateWebpage>BreakWebpageCache>BanVarnishWebpage`; a queued job starts the chain at its own class), counted in Redis per ban and stored once per purge. Group by `ban_sources` keys to see which kind of change keeps wiping a site before deciding what else should skip the edge. The purge is by cache tag, never by host: DetectIrisWebsite sets `Cache-Tag: aiku-website-<id>` on every storefront response (Varnish keeps it, Cloudflare reads and strips it), so purging the tag drops the pages and keeps assets and `/i/` images at the edge. A storefront response that skips the iris middleware group has no tag and is never purged, so keep it out of the edge cache rule. Pages that Varnish refreshes only by TTL, never by a ban (`/reviews` and `/customer-reviews`, 5 minutes in `set_reviews_ttl`), get no purge either, so they must keep a short edge TTL whatever the main rule says. Never purge per ban or per URL: on the Free plan, purging by tag or host is limited to 5 requests a minute per account (bucket of 25), and bans burst to 10k a minute; a 429 releases the job until Cloudflare's Retry-After. A new ban path must call `schedule()` too, or its change stays at the edge for the full edge TTL. Breaking a cache by hand (the website and webpage Break cache buttons, the `ban/varnish` links and the `website:break_cache`, `websites:break_cache`, `website:break_iris_cache`, and `varnish:*` commands) calls `purgeNow()` as well, which queues the purge at once instead of in 10 minutes; only those manual entry points (`asController`/`asCommand`) do, while `::run()` callers stay debounced. A deploy that flushes Varnish (SSR checksum changed) runs `php artisan cloudflare:purge-host` on each production host before the warm-up crawl: the first host purges every website's tag at once under a 2-minute lock, the others skip, and after a 429 the remaining sites are queued 5 a minute; deploys that keep the checksum purge nothing. `php artisan cloudflare:purge-host <slug>` purges one site at once. There are two Varnish instances: litio's HAProxy (`haproxy-litio.cfg`, the IP every storefront DNS record points at) uses litio's own Varnish, and boro's uses boro's. Bans reach both through `iris.cache.varnish_hosts`, and so does `varnish:restart` (and the `ban/varnish` link), which sends `x-ban-all` to every host; `restart_varnish.sh` only bans the Varnish on the host it runs on, which is why the deploy runs it on every host. On 1 Nov 2026, before `varnish:restart` used HTTP, flushing only boro left litio serving untagged pages that no tag purge could clear. Any new storefront path that must never be shared between visitors needs adding to that rule's exclusions as well as the VCL pass list. The rules live in `devops/cloudflare/edge-cache-rules.json`; apply or roll back a zone with `devops/cloudflare/edge-cache.sh <zone_id> <host> [--off]`, and change the JSON first, never only the zone. A new storefront zone needs the script run for its storefront host (the canonical host of the website's storefront webpage), or it serves every page from our servers.

## Only the reviews pages keep 10 minutes at the edge
Since 1 Nov 2026 the main edge rule keeps every anonymous page 24 hours (72 hours since 3 Nov 2026; Raul kept it at 72 rather than 7 days because the stale price in the JSON-LD is what Google's crawler compares with the Merchant Center feed), search engines and Google included, and a second rule after it gives 10 minutes only to `/reviews` and `/customer-reviews`: Varnish refreshes those by TTL (`set_reviews_ttl`), never by a ban, so no purge ever clears them. Search engines need no shorter TTL, because the edge is now exactly as fresh as Varnish: every change that bans a page in Varnish for what people see (page fields, webpage edits) purges it at the edge within 10 minutes. Price and stock in the anonymous copy can be up to 72 hours old (see above), for search engines too. The 24 h copy for far scraper countries (the `testa=far` rewrite) was removed the same night; Varnish still strips `testa` (the `#debug` entry in vcl_init), harmlessly. If a Google Ads or Merchant Center review ever sees an outdated page, `php artisan cloudflare:purge-host <slug>` clears that website from the edge at once. A path Varnish refreshes only by TTL needs adding to that second rule.

## Bots have their own Varnish copy, which deploy flushes spare
Since 4 Nov 2026 (INI-193), Varnish gives logged-out requests from known bot user agents their own copy of each page. The bot list is the `X-Aiku-Bot` regex in `vcl_recv`, the only place it lives. Search engines and `aiku-cache-warmer` are excluded and share the copy people get. Bot copies are tagged `X-Aiku-Bot-Copy: 1`, capped at 3 days, and spared by the deploy flush: `restart_varnish.sh` bans `obj.http.x-aiku-website ~ . && obj.http.x-aiku-bot-copy != 1`. An object without the header still matches `!=`, as checked with varnishtest. Every other ban still kills bot copies: webpage, URL, website and `x-ban-all` (`varnish:restart`). The reason: about 80% of Iris page renders came from bots re-crawling the long tail after each deploy flush, about 5 a day. A breaking deploy can't hurt a bot, because it runs no JavaScript. People keep the full flush, because old JS calling a changed backend is why the flush exists. A Cloudflare response rule never stores a response carrying `X-Aiku-Bot-Copy`: Free has no custom cache key, so a stored bot copy would reach people after a deploy purge. Never change the deploy ban to spare anything people see.

Crawlers that are never worth rendering for are blocked at Cloudflare. Each of the 29 storefront zones has one firewall custom rule, `aiku iris: blocked crawlers (INI-193)`, whose action is block. Since 4 Nov 2026 it blocks any `lower(http.user_agent)` that contains one of: screaming frog, ahrefs, dotbot, mj12bot, awariobot, barkrowler, dataforseobot, bytespider, sqlspider, shapbot, tphotobot, elevenlabscrawler. Free plans have no regex, so the expression is a chain of `contains` checks. Screaming Frog was our own Bali office: it crawled every storefront daily from 30 Oct, peaking at 237k requests on 4 Nov, about half of all bot renders. The SEO team has aiku's own Site Audit (`AuditWebsite`, AikuSiteAuditBot) for this. The rest are SEO data sellers and crawlers that bring no visitors, together about a quarter of the remaining bot renders. Never block search engines, AI assistants (ClaudeBot, GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Amazonbot), product importers, `curl` or `php-requests`: those can bring sales, or are our own scripts or customers' imports. To block another crawler, extend that rule's expression on every zone rather than adding a rule. Free plans allow 5 custom rules per zone, and awartisan.pt and aw-dropship.com are already at 5, so `cloudflare:block-countries` would fail there. `BlockCountriesInCloudflare` keeps rules that are not its own, so the two live side by side.

## Tracking parameters are dropped before the edge cache
Since 3 Nov 2026 a URL rewrite in each storefront zone (the `http_request_transform` rule in `devops/cloudflare/edge-cache-rules.json`) removes the tracking parameters Varnish strips (`remove_query_args`: gclid, gbraid, wbraid, gad_*, fbclid, msclkid, the utm_* names and the rest of the VCL regex list) before Cloudflare looks up its copy, so an ad or newsletter landing shares the page's edge copy instead of always missing. Free plans have no custom cache key, and the function takes exact names, so the utm_*, mc_* and _bta_* globs in the VCL are covered only by the names the VCL also lists one by one; the edge never strips more than Varnish. Traffic sources do not depend on the page request: Varnish already strips these before the app, and attribution is captured from the browser's `/json` first-hit call (the address-bar query in X-Traffic-Sources, the page URL as its Referer, document.referrer as X-Original-Referer), while the Google tag and Meta pixel read gclid and fbclid from the address bar, which a rewrite never changes. A parameter added to the VCL strip lists must be added to this rule too, and a real page parameter (page, search, filters) must never be. The rewrite only runs on the paths the edge caches: it carries the cache rule's path exclusions (`/app`, `/json`, `/models`, `/api` and the rest), just as the VCL passes those paths before it strips. From 3 to 5 Nov 2026 it ran on every path and removed `origin=bundles` from Retina's `/app/.../filtered-products` calls, so Create bundle hid every product already in My Products (PRE-68). Keep the two exclusion lists identical.
