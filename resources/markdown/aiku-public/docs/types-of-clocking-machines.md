<aside class="tldr">
A <em>clocking machine</em> is whatever your staff touch to say "I'm here" and "I'm leaving" — a tablet by the door, a printed QR code on the wall, a dedicated device. Each machine lives in a <b>workplace</b> and has a <b>type</b> that decides how people clock with it. The four everyday types — <b>PIN</b>, <b>Barcode Scanner</b>, <b>Camera QR</b> and <b>QR Code</b> — need nothing more than a tablet or the employees' own phones. Every clock-in it records lands on the employee's timesheet automatically.
</aside>

## Why machines at all?

aiku does not just want to know *that* someone clocked in — it wants to know *where* and *how*. Tying every clocking to a named machine in a named workplace means the timesheets can tell the warehouse door from the office door, and you can retire a device without losing the history it recorded. That is why the first step is always "create the machine in aiku", even when the "machine" is nothing more than a laminated QR code.

## The everyday types

These four are the ones you can create yourself from the clocking machines list, and none of them needs special hardware.

### PIN

A shared tablet by the entrance shows a keypad. Each employee has their own short personal PIN; they type it and they are clocked in — type it again later and they are clocked out. Employees can see their own PIN on the **Employee Clocking** page in aiku.

Best when: you want the simplest possible shared device and staff can be trusted not to share PINs.

### Barcode Scanner

The same shared tablet, but instead of typing, each employee scans their personal barcode — shown on their own **Employee Clocking** page, so it can live on their phone screen or be printed on a badge. A cheap USB or Bluetooth barcode scanner plugged into the tablet does the reading.

Best when: staff already carry badges, or you want clocking to take one beep instead of four taps.

### Camera QR Scanner

Again a shared tablet, but this time the tablet's own **camera** does the work: the employee holds up the QR code from their phone's Employee Clocking page, the camera reads it, done. No extra scanner hardware needed.

Best when: you want scan-speed clocking with nothing but a tablet.

### QR Code

This one turns the logic around: instead of a shared device reading the employee's code, the **employee's own phone** reads a code on the wall. You print one or more QR codes for the machine and stick them by the door; the employee scans one with their phone camera, which opens aiku's Employee Clocking page, and — signed in as themselves — they clock in or out.

A QR Code machine has two tricks the others don't:

- **Location matching.** You can require the phone to share its location and only accept clockings within a radius (in metres) of a point you pick on a map — so nobody clocks in "at the office" from their sofa.
- **Clocking policies.** Each machine can carry rules that mark an employee's clocking as **onsite**, **remote** or **hybrid**, so working from home can be allowed for some people without switching off the location check for everyone.

Best when: there is no spare tablet, staff all have phones, or people work across several doors and sites.

## How to choose

- One door, one spare tablet → **PIN** to start; upgrade to **Camera QR** when typing gets old.
- Staff carry badges or you own a scanner → **Barcode Scanner**.
- No spare device at all → **QR Code** on the wall, and let phones do the work.
- You need to be sure people are physically on site → **QR Code** with location matching turned on.

Whatever you pick, nothing is final: a machine's type shapes how people clock, but the clockings all end up in the same place — the employee's timesheet — and you can add a second machine of a different type to the same workplace any time.
