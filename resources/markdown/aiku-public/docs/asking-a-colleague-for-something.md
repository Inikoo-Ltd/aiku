<aside class="tldr">
A <b>task</b> is a request from one member of staff to another, or to a whole department: update this banner, check this product against its picture, send the missing invoice. It is not a ticket, tickets are for things that are broken in aiku and go to the engineers. A task has one owner, a chat thread, and ends in <b>Done</b> or <b>Can't be done</b>. Two things are required to raise one: what needs doing and who you are asking.
</aside>

## Why tasks and not Slack

A request written in Slack has no owner and no end. It scrolls away, nobody knows if it was seen, and a week later someone asks again. A task is the same sentence with an owner and a done, so both sides can stop worrying about it. The discussion still happens in chat: every task opens a staff chat conversation named after it, with the same translation, replies, photos and quick replies described in [Talking to colleagues in staff chat](/docs/staff-chat).

## Raising a task

Open <b>Tasks</b> in the left menu and press <b>New task</b>. Type what needs doing, then choose either a <b>person</b> or a <b>department</b>. That is all. Details, a due date and an urgency are there if you need them, but most tasks do not.

Two shortcuts save the trip to the Tasks page:

- <b>From the page it is about.</b> Product, customer, order and delivery note pages have a <b>Task</b> button in their header. A task raised there is linked to that record, so whoever picks it up lands on the right product or order with one click, and the page shows a chip for every open task about it.
- <b>From a chat message.</b> Hover any message in staff chat and press <b>task</b>. The message becomes the subject, and a reply in the original conversation says which task was raised, so the person who asked can follow it.

## Person or department

Ask a <b>person</b> when you know who should do it. They get the task straight away and it shows under <b>Assigned to me</b> on their Tasks page.

Ask a <b>department</b> when you do not, for example the warehouse or marketing. The task sits in that department's queue, visible to everyone in it under <b>My department</b>. Whoever picks it up presses <b>I will do it</b> and becomes the owner. Departments come from job positions, nothing to set up.

Engineers and QA cannot be given a task. Anything for them is a ticket, see [Asking the help desk for help](/docs/asking-the-help-desk-for-help).

## Working through tasks sent to you

The Tasks page has three views:

- <b>Assigned to me</b>: tasks that are yours.
- <b>My department</b>: the queue of your department, waiting for someone to pick them up.
- <b>I asked for</b>: tasks you raised, so you can see how they are getting on.

Each task has four buttons: <b>I will do it</b> to claim it, <b>Working on it</b>, <b>Done</b>, and <b>Can't be done</b>, which asks you for a short reason. Every change is posted in the task's thread, so the person who asked sees it without being told. The thread icon opens the conversation to ask a question, attach a photo or say it is finished.

<b>Show closed</b> lists what was finished or dropped.

## When nothing happens

A task that has been quiet for two days gets a nudge: the owner is reminded, or if nobody has claimed it yet, the supervisors of the department are told it is waiting. The reminder repeats every two days until the task moves. There is nothing to configure and no other deadline; a due date on a task is information for the person doing it, not a rule.

## The board and the reports

<b>Board</b> shows the same tasks as columns, Todo, Working on it, Done, Can't be done, for the period chosen at the top, with filters by department, urgency and assignee. Supervisors can drag a card from one column to another to change its status; everyone else sees the board read only.

<b>Reports</b> shows, for the chosen period, how many tasks were raised and done, how long they take, how many have been open for more than a week, and the same numbers by department, by assignee and by requester. <b>All</b> is the plain list of every task with filters and search.

<aside class="wayfinder"><strong>Where to click in aiku</strong>
<ul>
<li><b>Raise a task:</b> <b>Tasks</b> → <b>New task</b>, or the <b>Task</b> button on a product, customer, order or delivery note page, or hover a chat message → <b>task</b>.</li>
<li><b>See what is yours:</b> <b>Tasks</b> → <b>Assigned to me</b>.</li>
<li><b>Pick up a task for your department:</b> <b>Tasks</b> → <b>My department</b> → <b>I will do it</b>.</li>
<li><b>Finish one:</b> <b>Done</b>, or <b>Can't be done</b> and say why.</li>
<li><b>Talk about it:</b> the thread icon on the task, or the conversation named after it in the messaging bar.</li>
</ul>
</aside>

<aside class="permissions"><strong>Permissions you need</strong>
Anyone who can log in to aiku can raise tasks, be given tasks, and see the Tasks page, board and reports. Engineers and QA see everything but cannot be assigned a task. Supervisors, anyone with a supervisor job position, can move cards on the board; for everyone else the board is read only. Which departments you can pick up tasks for follows your job positions.
</aside>
