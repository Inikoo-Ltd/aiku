<aside class="tldr">
aiku always shows your notifications inside aiku. On <strong>Edit profile → Notifications</strong> you pick where else each one should reach you: <strong>Email</strong>, <strong>Slack</strong> or <strong>Browser</strong>. Browser notifications pop up on your screen like a message from any other app, even when no aiku tab is open, but you have to switch them on once on every computer and phone you use.
</aside>

## What you can be notified about

The **Notify me when** grid lists the moments worth interrupting you for:

- **My ticket is resolved** - an engineer has finished the ticket you raised.
- **New comment on my ticket** - someone wrote on your ticket.
- **Someone @mentions me on a ticket** - a colleague typed your username or nickname after an @.
- **My ticket needs more information from me** - an engineer asked you a question, and the ticket is cancelled if nobody answers in time.

For each one, tick the channels you want. Leave a row empty and that notification only appears inside aiku. Click **Save** when you are done.

## The three channels

- **Email** - sent to the email address at the top of the same page. Greyed out until you add one.
- **Slack** - a direct message from the aiku bot. Greyed out until you add your Slack member ID (in Slack, open your profile → **⋮** → **Copy member ID**).
- **Browser** - a notification from your computer's or phone's own notification system. Clicking it opens the ticket.

## Switching on browser notifications

Browser notifications belong to a *device*, not to your account, so you switch them on once on each computer and phone:

1. Open aiku on that device and go to **Edit profile → Notifications**.
2. Click **Enable browser notifications on this device**.
3. Your browser asks whether aiku may show notifications. Click **Allow**.
4. The button changes to **Browser notifications are on for this device**.
5. Tick **Browser** on the rows you want, then **Save**.

If you switch devices later, repeat the steps on the new one. Every device you enable gets the notification.

### On an iPhone or iPad

Apple only allows web notifications from apps on your home screen:

1. Open aiku in **Safari**.
2. Tap **Share** → **Add to Home Screen**.
3. Open aiku from the new home-screen icon, not from Safari.
4. Follow the steps above.

### On Android

Chrome on Android works like a computer: open aiku, enable, and allow.

## When nothing pops up

Work down this list:

- **The browser blocked aiku.** If the Browser column is greyed out, or the address bar shows *Notifications blocked*, click that icon (or the settings icon to the left of the address), set **Notifications** to **Allow**, and reload the page.
- **Your computer is blocking the browser.** This is the most common reason, and the browser itself cannot tell you.
  - **On a Mac:** open **System Settings → Notifications** and scroll down the list of apps. Chrome is listed as **Google Chrome**, under **G**, not under C. Searching for "Chrome" in the settings search box does not find it. Click it, turn on **Allow notifications**, and set the style to **Banners** or **Alerts**. If **Google Chrome** appears twice, turn on both.
  - **On Windows:** open **Settings → System → Notifications** and turn on notifications for **Google Chrome** (or your browser).
- **Focus or Do Not Disturb is on.** Notifications arrive silently in the notification centre until you turn it off.
- **Browser is not ticked** on that row, or you did not click **Save**.
- **On iPhone**, you opened aiku from Safari instead of the home-screen icon.

## Switching them off

- **Stop one kind of notification:** untick **Browser** on that row and **Save**.
- **Stop them on one device:** open the page on that device and click **Turn off**. Your other devices keep receiving them.
- **A device you no longer have:** nothing to do. Once its browser stops accepting notifications, aiku forgets it after the next attempt.
