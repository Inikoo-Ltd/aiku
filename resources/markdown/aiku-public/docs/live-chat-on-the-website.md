<aside class="tldr">
During working hours, a message in the bubble lands in your <b>Customer Inbox</b> like any other conversation. Outside them, the widget takes a name, an email and a message instead. Where the shop has <b>Answer offline messages by email</b> switched on, that message is moved to the <b>email</b> channel, so your answer is sent to the customer's inbox rather than into a widget they have already closed. That is why you will sometimes find a website visitor in the email list.
</aside>

## During working hours

The bubble opens a live conversation. It joins <b>Waiting</b> until somebody takes it, and from there it is answered like everything else in Chat - see <a href="/docs/customer-chat">Talking to customers in Chat</a>.

## Outside working hours

Working hours come from the shop's work schedule and the organisation's public holidays, both kept in <b>Human Resources</b>. Outside them, the widget tells the visitor when you are back and asks for their name, email and message rather than leaving them in an empty chat.

What happens to that message depends on one switch in the shop's settings, <b>Answer offline messages by email</b>:

- <b>Off</b> - the message stays a website conversation. It waits in the website list, and your answer is written into the same widget. If the visitor has closed the tab, they only see it if they come back and open the widget.
- <b>On</b> - the conversation moves to the <b>email</b> channel. It waits in the email list, your answer is sent from the shop's mailbox, and their reply comes back into the same conversation. If the visitor already had a website conversation open, that one moves to email too. The switch does nothing until a mailbox is connected, so an answer is never sent nowhere.

Whether a shop uses it is the chat supervisor's decision. Most visitors who write out of hours have left long before anyone answers, which is why email reaches far more of them.

## Spotting an out-of-hours message

Inside the conversation, the customer's message shows a small <b>Offline message</b> label. The row in the list has no mark, so in the email list these look like any other email until you open them.

## Emailing a customer who is not there any more

During working hours, a customer sometimes asks a question and closes the tab before you answer. <b>&#8942;</b> &rarr; <b>Email notification</b> set to <b>On</b> also sends what you write to them by email. It appears only in a conversation you are holding, where there is an address to write to, and never on an email conversation, which is already an email.
