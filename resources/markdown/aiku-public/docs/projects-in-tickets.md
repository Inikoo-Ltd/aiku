<aside class="tldr">
A big piece of work - a warehouse move, say - is many tickets and tasks. A <b>project</b> keeps them together with a goal, a team, milestones and a progress bar. Anyone can see where it stands, and the team posts <b>progress updates</b> with a health mark on the project page instead of sending chat messages.
</aside>

## Finding projects

In the left menu open <b>Tickets</b>, then <b>Projects</b> in the top menu. Every project is a card showing its health, status, progress bar, owner and team. Click one to open it.

## Starting a project

Press <b>New project</b> and fill in <b>Name</b>, <b>Goal</b> (what done looks like), <b>Start</b>, <b>Target</b> (the date you want it finished), <b>Owner</b> and <b>Team</b>. Press <b>Create</b>. Anyone can start a project.

## The project page

At the top a strip shows the <b>Health</b> (the mark from the latest update that has one), the <b>Status</b>, the <b>Owner and team</b>, the progress bars, and the counts <b>Open</b>, <b>Done</b> and <b>Days left</b>. The buttons <b>New ticket</b> and <b>New task</b> create work that is already in the project. <b>Edit</b> changes the project details (only for people who can change the project, see below).

The page has five tabs: <b>Overview</b>, <b>Work</b>, <b>Timeline</b>, <b>Commits</b> and <b>Activity</b>.

## Putting work in

Tickets and tasks count together. On the <b>Work</b> tab:

- <b>Paste references:</b> in the box type the references, for example HELP-123, TASK-45 - several at once, tickets and tasks mixed, is fine. Optionally pick a milestone, then press <b>Add</b>.
- <b>New ticket</b> or <b>New task:</b> at the top of the page.
- <b>From a ticket or task:</b> the side panel has a <b>Project</b> chip and a milestone chip. Click to pick a project or milestone, or choose <b>No project</b> or <b>No milestone</b> to clear it.

To take an item out from the project page, click the <b>x</b> next to it.

## Work tab

Search, and filter by <b>Tickets and tasks</b>, milestone, person (<b>Everyone</b> or <b>Unassigned</b>), and tick <b>Show closed</b> to include finished work. Switch between <b>List</b> and <b>Board</b> (columns <b>Todo</b>, <b>In progress</b>, <b>Done</b>, and <b>Cancelled</b> when closed work is shown). In the list, work is grouped by milestone and each row has its own milestone selector.

Work you are not allowed to see is not listed; the page says how many confidential items are hidden.

## Overview tab

- <b>Goal</b> of the project.
- <b>Progress updates:</b> write what moved, what is blocked and what comes next, optionally mark <b>On track</b>, <b>At risk</b> or <b>Off track</b>, then press <b>Post update</b>. Updates stay in order, newest first. The owner and the rest of the team are told straight away, in the Aiku bell and by email; the person who posted is not.
- <b>Milestones:</b> a checklist with start and due dates and a small progress bar per milestone (items done out of items). Tick one to finish it; rename, reorder or remove with the small controls. Past its due date and not ticked it is marked <b>Overdue</b>.
- <b>Workload:</b> per person, how many items are todo, in progress and done.

## Timeline, Commits and Activity

- <b>Timeline:</b> the milestones along a calendar with a <b>Today</b> line, and a <b>Burn-up</b> chart comparing all the work (<b>Scope</b>) with what is <b>Done</b> week by week and an <b>Ideal</b> line.
- <b>Commits:</b> the code changes that fixed the project's tickets, with <b>Version</b>, <b>Deployed</b>, <b>Commit</b>, <b>Subject</b> and the <b>Ticket</b>. They appear by themselves when a ticket's fix is deployed.
- <b>Activity:</b> what happened, newest first: work added or finished, updates, deployments. It is built automatically.

## Reading progress

The progress bars show how much of the work is done and how far we are between start and target. The work bar turns red when it is more than 10 points behind the time. Past the target date it counts the days over. Cancelled work is left out of the totals.

## Through the AI assistant

You can also ask the AI assistant connected to Aiku to do it for you, in plain words: "show me the project", "put HELP-123 and TASK-45 under the first milestone", "move the date of the second milestone to next Friday", "post an update: at risk, the supplier is late". It acts as you, with the same rights you have on the page.

## Who can change what

The owner, the team members, and engineers who can assign tickets can edit the project (<b>Edit</b>, add or remove work, post updates, change milestones). Everyone else can read it.

Moving a single ticket or task in or out of a project from its own side panel is also open to the people working on it: for a ticket, the people who can update it and its collaborators; for a task, the assignee, collaborators and the person who asked for it. Anyone else can only do it if they can change both the project it is in and the one it is going to.

<aside class="wayfinder"><strong>Where to click in aiku</strong>
<ul>
<li><b>See projects:</b> <b>Tickets</b> &rarr; <b>Projects</b>.</li>
<li><b>Start one:</b> <b>New project</b> &rarr; fill in the form &rarr; <b>Create</b>.</li>
<li><b>Add existing work:</b> open the project &rarr; <b>Work</b> &rarr; type references &rarr; <b>Add</b>.</li>
<li><b>Add new work:</b> open the project &rarr; <b>New ticket</b> or <b>New task</b>.</li>
<li><b>Move work in or out:</b> open the ticket or task &rarr; <b>Project</b> chip in the side panel.</li>
<li><b>Report progress:</b> open the project &rarr; <b>Overview</b> &rarr; write in the box &rarr; <b>Post update</b>.</li>
</ul>
